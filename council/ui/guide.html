The Guide

Getting Started

The Council is a panel of AI analysts that each study one slice of a stock's data, argue it out, and say which way they lean on the stock over the next week, the next 3 months and the next year -- and how sure they are.

  1. Add a key. Go to Settings → API Keys and add either an Anthropic key (best answers, paid) or a free Gemini key. Each comes with step-by-step instructions -- no technical knowledge needed. Stock data is free and needs no key.
  2. Going free? After adding the Gemini key, turn on Free Mode under Settings → Models & Cost. Read its trade-offs first: free answers are weaker than Claude's.
  3. Ask about a stock. In the Chamber, type a ticker (like NVDA) and press Convene the Council. Every run covers all three terms at once.
  4. Watch the Council work. Each seat reads its own data and leans a way on each term. Tap any seat to see its reasoning.
  5. Read the result. Three bars -- short, medium and long term -- show where the Council leans and how strongly, with any warnings under them. The Grand Master explains each one. Every run is saved in the Crypt.

Reading a Verdict

BULLISH
expects the price to rise
BEARISH
expects the price to fall
DEAD EVEN
read its data and found it genuinely cancels out (shown as NO_CONVICTION in the Crypt)
NO_READ
couldn't form a read at all -- the AI call failed, its answer came back malformed, or the seat had no data

This mapping is the same everywhere -- seat chairs, the centerpiece, the bars, and every seat's lean in the Crypt. Seats are asked to lean one way whenever the evidence tilts at all, and to be honest about how much: a weak lean sits close to 50%. Dead even is only for evidence that truly cancels out, and it pulls the Council's position toward the middle. NO_READ means something kept the seat from reading at all -- it's left out of the position entirely; tap the seat to see why.

Three Terms, Three Bars

One piece of news can point different ways over different spans -- an acquisition can weigh on a stock this week and help it next year -- so every run covers three terms at once:

  • Short term -- the next week. Checked in the Crypt after 7 days.
  • Medium term -- the next 3 months. Checked after 91 days.
  • Long term -- the next year and beyond. Seats may reason years out; the Crypt checks it after a year.

Each term gets a bar running from bearish to bullish. The glowing marker is the Council's position: the average of every seat's lean, weighted by how much that seat's data says about that term (see Seats by Term). The thin ticks are the seats themselves -- taller ticks count for more. The label above the bar puts the lean in words: Barely, Leaning, plain, or Strongly bullish or bearish, or Dead even. A marker near the middle is close to a coin flip, and is drawn that way on purpose.

Amber notes under a bar are warnings, never overrides: too few seats could read their data, most seats expect a bigger move than the stock usually makes, the expected move doesn't clear trading costs, or the Prosecutor objected to that term's case.

The Chamber

  1. Type a stock ticker, e.g. NVDA.
  2. Pick Full or Lite. Full has every seat answer three times and runs two debate rounds (43 model calls); Lite has each seat answer once with one debate round (16 calls) -- faster and cheaper, a little less thorough. Lite is the default in Free Mode, since free daily limits go about 2.5× further.
  3. Optionally, ask a question (e.g. "Is the recent capex guidance already priced in?"). It only shapes the Grand Master's final summary -- every seat still reads its own data for the ticker and votes independently.
  4. Press Convene the Council. Each seat's bar fills as it gathers its data and works through its samples, then settles into the colour of its overall lean -- brighter the stronger the lean. The three pips on each seat (S, M, L) show its lean on each term. Click or tap any seat to read its reasoning per term.
  5. Once every seat has reported: the Reality Anchor appears, then the Bull/Bear debate with the Prosecutor's rulings, then the three bars with any warnings, and finally the Grand Master's notes. The centerpiece shows all three terms at a glance. The whole run is saved to the Crypt automatically.

No keys added yet? The Council still runs, but only replays built-in sample answers about NVDA. Add a key in Settings → API Keys to analyze any stock for real.

Out of free runs or credit? In Free Mode, when Gemini's daily limit runs out mid-run the Council switches to Groq on its own (if you've added a Groq key) and tells you. If nothing is left -- or a paid account runs out of credit -- the run stops, says when the limit resets or links to where to add credit, and nothing from that run is saved.

The Crypt

  1. Every run ever made lives here, permanently -- the ledger is append-only and hash-chained, so nothing can be edited or quietly deleted after the fact.
  2. Each card is one run with a line per term. Each term is scored on its own: the short term a week after the run, the medium term after 3 months, the long term after a year.
  3. Free Mode runs are tagged FREE and Lite runs LITE. Filter by ticker, term and/or run type, then click Update the Crypt to score every term whose window has passed against what actually happened.
  4. Click a card to see the three bars, the Grand Master's notes, and every seat's lean on each term.
  5. The scoreboard compares the Council's blind vote, final (weighted) vote, and extremized vote against two baselines: buy-and-hold and always-bullish.

The Archives

  1. Once real resolutions accumulate in the Crypt, this shows each seat's actual track record: hit rate, Brier score, log loss, and a calibration curve (predicted probability vs. how often that was actually right).
  2. Paid and Free Mode runs are scored separately -- pick Paid runs, Free runs or All at the top -- so cheaper free-model runs never drag down, or prop up, the paid Council's record.
  3. Rank (Youngling → Grand Master) requires at least 20 resolved predictions before it can move at all -- a lucky streak cannot mint a Grand Master, and a seat's Crypt-derived weight in future votes only starts shifting once it clears that same floor.

Settings

  1. API Keys -- connect the Council to the AI services it uses. Each key comes with step-by-step instructions for getting it.
  2. Models & Cost -- turn Free Mode on or off, choose the AI model each seat runs on, and see the estimated cost of a Full or Lite run. Saved on the server, so it applies to every deliberation on this copy of the Council.
  3. Appearance -- pick the centerpiece and the seat-chair style (including the pixel-art Seat Wizards). Purely visual, saved to your browser only.

How Much Each Seat Counts, by Term

Every seat leans on every term -- none is ever left out -- but some data says more about some spans than others: charts, options and news are short-term evidence, fundamentals and macro are long-term. This weight (0-1) sets how much each seat's lean counts toward each term's position, before data-quality and track-record adjustments are applied on top.

Tier I -- The Twelve Seats

Every seat below deliberates in total isolation -- it is constructed with only the data fields listed, enforced in code (not by prompting), and is sampled several times (three by default) to measure its own run-to-run consistency before its votes are combined with anyone else's.

Keeper of the Charts
technician
Sees: price/volume (OHLCV) only. The ticker itself is anonymised to "Instrument A" so the model can't pattern-match the real name from training data.
Does: its short-term lean is decided by a deterministic family-vote engine (trend, breakout, oscillator, volume), never by the LLM, which only narrates the setup and places entry/exit/invalidation from ATR and structure. Its medium and long-term leans are its read of the longer trend, and count for less.
Keeper of the Ledgers
fundamentalist
Sees: financial statements, margins, growth, and valuation multiples vs. sector. No price action at all.
Does: a fundamentals read. Counts most on the long term and very little on the short term.
Watcher of Omens
catalyst_seer
Sees: the news/sentiment feed only.
Does: classifies each catalyst SCHEDULED vs SURPRISE and must justify why a headline isn't already priced in.
Student of the Inner Circle
insider_reader
Sees: Form 4 insider transactions only.
Does: weighs cluster buys over single buys, C-suite over directors, open-market purchases far over option exercises; treats routine 10b5-1 sales as near-zero signal.
Reader of the Republic
senate_watcher
Sees: the stock trades House and Senate members disclosed in this company over the last year -- who, party and state, buy or sell, the dollar range, and whose account. Needs a free Alpha Vantage key (Settings → API Keys).
Does: weighs buys over sells, several members trading together, and the disclosure lag -- trades are filed up to 45 days after they happen.
Reader of Great Tides
flow_cartographer
Sees: 13F institutional holdings, ETF inclusion, short interest, days-to-cover.
Does: must note the quarterly lag on 13F data and discount its own confidence accordingly.
Reader of Probabilities
oracle_options
Sees: the option chain / IV surface / put-call ratio only.
Does: its most valuable output is the implied expected move, not direction -- every other seat's price target gets checked against this seat's distribution downstream.
Keeper of the Outer Rim
macro_sage
Sees: yields, curve shape, Fed path proxies, CPI, employment, the dollar, oil, and the ticker's historical beta to the market -- never the ticker's own price series.
Does: a macro backdrop read. Counts most on the medium and long terms; its short-term lean is usually weak.
Reader of Distant Stars
cross_market
Sees: the sector ETF, a peer basket, an index-futures proxy, and an overseas-session proxy -- never the ticker's own price series.
Does: looks for confirmation (or contradiction) from a genuinely different market, on the theory that same-asset data is already priced in.
Keeper of Expectations
estimate_scribe
Sees: analyst earnings estimates, revision history, guidance vs. consensus, historical surprise pattern.
Does: weighs revision momentum more heavily than the raw estimate level.
Reader of the Guild's Targets
analyst_ratings
Sees: Wall Street's published price targets (as upside from today's price, never the price itself), how many analysts cover the stock, the buy/hold/sell split now and three months ago, and recent upgrades and downgrades.
Does: discounts analysts' usual optimism, weighs changes over levels, and treats a rare downgrade as stronger news than a routine upgrade. Targets are 12-month calls, so it counts most on the medium and long terms.
Keeper of Charters
structure_archivist
Sees: SEC filings (8-K, S-1, S-3, 13D/G), buybacks, dilution, debt maturities, covenants, ESG/litigation flags.
Does: low frequency, high impact -- its leans are often weak, and that's correct behaviour for this seat, not a failure.
Tier II -- The Adversarial Chamber
Bull Advocate & Bear Advocate
bull_advocate / bear_advocate
Sees: all twelve seats' leans on each term, their theses and data quality -- never the raw underlying data.
Does: each builds the strongest possible case for its assigned side regardless of personal read, saying which terms each point bears on. The assignment is fixed, not chosen -- they are advocates, not analysts.
The Devil's Advocate
prosecutor
Sees: everything -- every seat's leans, the debate so far, and which way the council currently leans on each term.
Does: attacks those leans, whichever way they point. Hunts for evidence cited by multiple seats (counted once, not once per seat), expected moves outside the plausible range, and theses that don't actually depend on the data shown. Its veto appears as a warning under the bars of the terms it objects to.
Tier III -- Quality Control

None of these four are an LLM call -- pure computation, deterministic, no token cost.

Keeper of What Usually Happens
base_rate_keeper
Computes: the ticker's unconditional historical return distribution -- hit rate of "up," realised volatility, ATR-implied range, max plausible move.
Used for: the Reality Anchor, per term -- every seat's expected move is checked against it; moves far outside it are flagged IMPLAUSIBLE and count for less.
Keeper of the Toll
cost_auditor
Computes: spread as a percentage of expected move, round-trip cost, break-even move.
Does: if a term's expected move is smaller than the cost of trading it, that term gets a warning under its bar.
Keeper of the Shields
risk_warden
Computes: ATR/IV-based position size for a fixed risk budget, with a fractional-Kelly cap and a concentration check against other open Crypt positions.
Does: sizing only, never direction -- its own function signature takes no vote or probability as input, so it is structurally incapable of sizing based on conviction.
Keeper of the Crypt's Memory
calibration_officer
Computes: from the resolved Crypt, per-seat hit rate, Brier score, log loss, and a calibration curve (what The Archives page displays).
Does: outputs a weight vector for the next deliberation's vote -- every seat starts at 1.0, and weight can only move once a seat has at least 20 resolved predictions, so a lucky streak can't buy influence early.
Tier IV -- Synthesis
Master of the Order
grand_master
Sees: everything -- every seat's leans, the full Bull/Bear debate, every Prosecutor ruling, the Reality Anchors and each term's warnings.
Does: explains the three positions -- a headline and a note per term on what drives the lean and how much to trust it. It never moves a position: those come from the seats' weighted leans, so a weak lean stays visibly weak. Must report the structure of disagreement, not just a consensus number -- unanimity among seats that all leaned on the same correlated evidence is treated as a warning sign, not a green light.
