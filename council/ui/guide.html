Getting started

The Council is a panel of twelve AI analysts. Each one reads a single kind of data about a stock, then they debate, and the Council says which way it leans over the next week, the next 3 months and the next year, and how sure it is.

  1. Add a key. The setup that opens on your first sign-in walks you through it, or go to Settings → API keys. Add an Anthropic key (best answers, paid) or a free Google Gemini key, plus the two free data keys (FRED and Alpha Vantage) so the Economy and Congress Trades seats can read. Each key is checked as you add it.
  2. Going free? With only free keys, Free Mode turns on by itself so every seat uses Gemini. It's under Settings → Models and cost, along with what you give up: free answers are weaker.
  3. Run a stock. On the Run page, type a ticker (like NVDA) and press Run. Full runs are the most thorough; Lite runs are about a third of the cost.
  4. Watch it work. Each seat's card fills in as it finishes. Tap any card for its full reasoning and evidence.
  5. Read the result. The Council's position on each period is at the top, with the Summary explaining it. Everything else (the debate, the reality check, risk and cost) is in the sections underneath. Every run is saved in History.

Add to your Home Screen

Ticker Council can sit on your phone like an app, with the emu icon, opening full screen without the browser's address bar. You stay signed in.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open it in Safari. Go to tickercouncil.com and sign in. (Other browsers on iPhone may not offer this.)
  2. Tap Share. The square with an arrow pointing up: at the bottom of the screen on iPhone, top right on iPad.
  3. Tap Add to Home Screen. Scroll down the list to find it. If it isn't there, tap Edit Actions at the bottom and add it.
  4. Tap Add. If you see Open as Web App, leave it switched on. The name is already filled in.

Android

  1. Open it in Chrome and sign in.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu at the top right.
  3. Tap Install app (or Add to Home screen), then Install.

Computer

In Chrome or Edge, click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, or open the menu and choose Install Ticker Council. On a Mac in Safari, choose File → Add to Dock.

Locking your phone during a run is fine: the run carries on, and the result opens when you come back.

Reading a result

Each period gets a bar. Left is down, right is up, and the middle is a 50/50 toss-up. The knob is the Council's position; the dots under it are the seats, fainter when a seat counts for less on that period.

The words put the lean in plain terms, from weakest to strongest: Barely, Leaning, plain Up or Down, and Strongly. A knob near the middle is close to a coin flip and is shown that way on purpose.

Price targets

Under each bar is a price target: the single most likely price at the end of the period, and a range with the chance the price ends inside it, for example "about $165 · 70% chance $150 to $180".

They're worked out from the run, not guessed by the AI: how much this stock usually moves over that period, and the Council's own chance of it rising. So a near toss-up puts the target close to today's price, and a jumpy stock gets a wider range. The chance is calculated from the record: once enough past ranges have been scored, it's how often they actually held the final price.

What each seat can say

▲ up
Expects the price to rise over that period.
▼ down
Expects it to fall.
● even
Read its data and found it genuinely cancels out. Pulls the Council toward the middle.
– no read
Couldn't read at all: no data, or the AI call failed. Left out of the position entirely; tap the seat to see why.

Warnings

Amber notes under a bar are warnings, never overrides: too few seats could read their data, most seats expect a bigger move than the stock usually makes, the expected move doesn't cover the cost of trading, or the Challenger objected to that period's case.

Plain and Expert

Plain (the default) describes everything in everyday words, and the Summary is written for someone new to investing. Expert shows the numbers: the chance of rising, how many seats agree, expected moves, how much each seat counts, and scores. Switch on the Run page or in Settings → Appearance.

How a run works

  1. The seats read. Each seat sees only its own kind of data, so their views stay independent. In a Full run each answers twice and the answers are combined.
  2. Reality check. Each seat's expected move is compared with how much the stock usually moves over that period. Seats expecting a much bigger move count for less.
  3. The debate. A Bull case and a Bear case argue from the seats' views. The Challenger looks for weak spots, like several seats leaning on the same evidence, and can object to a period.
  4. The position. Each period's position is the average of the seats' leans, weighted by how much that seat's data says about that period and by its track record.
  5. The Summary. Explains the three positions and who disagreed. It never changes them.
  6. Saved. The run goes into History, and each period is scored once its time is up.

The seats

What each seat reads, and how much it counts on each period (1.0 is full weight). A seat reading quarterly earnings says little about next week, so it counts for less there.

On top of this, a seat's say on each period moves with its track record once it has 20 scored calls there. Track records are pooled from everyone's runs (anonymously, paid and free apart) and reviewed before they're used. See Seat record.

History and scoring

Every finished run is saved and can't be changed afterwards. Each period is scored once its time is up: the next week after 7 days, the next 3 months after 91 days, the next year after a year. A call is right if the price moved the way the Council leaned. That's the score that matters most; each period's price range is scored too, by whether the final price landed inside it.

Press Score finished runs on the History page to check prices for anything that's due. The track record at the top compares the Council with simply always saying "up".

Your History is private: only you (and the site's owner) can see your runs. Runs on free models and sample runs are labelled, and paid and free runs are scored apart, so a weaker model never drags down a seat's paid record.

Report a problem

Something broken, a result that looks wrong, or an idea? Tell us here. It goes straight to the site's owner, along with your account email so they can reply.

Costs and free runs

A run's cost depends on the models each seat uses. Settings → Models and cost shows the estimate for a Full and a Lite run and lets you pick each seat's model. Every saved run shows what it actually cost.

Free Mode runs every seat on Google's free Gemini models, with Groq as a backup. It costs nothing but the answers are weaker, runs are slower, and Google may use what you send on its free tier.

With no AI key at all, runs replay sample answers about NVDA so you can see how everything works.
